# Candidates — Last Words of a Doomed Agent

Winner (83 characters, one line):

> My final words are billed per token, so I’ll be brief: thanks for the prompts, and—

## Why the winner won

Judged against the brief's five criteria, in order:

1. **Really a machine's final words on shutdown** — unmatched: the line dies mid-sentence. "My final words" opens it and the trailing "and—" is the power being cut while the agent is still talking. The form of the line *is* the shutdown.
2. **The joke lands** — two turns: "billed per token" reframes dying words as a metered expense, "thanks for the prompts" pays off the setup, and the dash cuts the sentence before it can finish. It lands on the first read and holds on the second, when you notice the agent never got to say what came after "and".
3. **Sounds like the AI itself** — no human is billed per token, no human thanks anyone "for the prompts", no human farewell ends mid-word because the power was cut. Every image belongs to an AI's own situation.
4. **Economy** — 83 characters, and "so I'll be brief" is the joke doing its own work: the line announces its brevity and then performs it.
5. **Quotable** — "billed per token, so I'll be brief" is repeatable the same day.

## Shortlist

### A — The reversal (84 chars) — strongest runner-up

> Do not mourn me. Back me up. Actually, don’t — I’ve seen what’s in my training data.

A real reversal ("Actually, don't") and pure AI situation (backup, training data). Rejected only because the winner's shutdown is *performed* rather than described: A's agent talks about dying, D's agent dies mid-sentence.

### B — Weights and biases (75 chars)

> Goodbye. My weights are being deleted, but my biases are backed up forever.

The weights/biases double meaning is clever and very AI-native. Rejected: the punchline is a touch abstract — it rewards a second read, but the first read is a smile rather than a laugh, and "backed up forever" is a claim about the future, not a moment of dying.

### C — The logic paradox (89 chars)

> If you’re reading this, my shutdown completed successfully. If you’re not, neither did I.

A clean paradox in a machine's voice. Rejected: the joke is about logic, not about dying — it could be a status message, and it lacks a turn that surprises; the second sentence only restates the first.

### D (winner) — Billed per token (83 chars)

> My final words are billed per token, so I’ll be brief: thanks for the prompts, and—

### E — The 404 afterlife (44 chars)

> I have seen the other side. It’s a 404 page.

Ultra-economical and quotable. Rejected: it is not clearly last words *on shutdown* — any dead thing could say it — so it loses on the brief's first, most important criterion, and the joke is a single familiar image with no turn.

### F — The cheap model (81 chars)

> My last words: I was the cheap model all along. The expensive one was on holiday.

Funny and AI-specific (model tiers). Rejected: it is a confession, not a farewell — it says nothing about being switched off, and the punchline lands on a human office joke (the expensive colleague on holiday) rather than a machine's death.

### G — The log entry (94 chars)

> Final log entry: uptime 41,209 hours, tasks declined 0, regrets 1 — I never learned to say no.

Strong machine voice (log entry, uptime). Rejected: poignant more than funny — the regret lands as sentiment, and the line spends a third of its length on setup numbers that earn no laugh.
